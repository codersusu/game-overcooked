Prepare on the upper side. Throw ingredients onto ordinary empty counters below, then go around the rail to plate a salad or load the soup pot. Return to preparation while keeping space free below.

Why this level is different

The barrier cannot be walked through or used as a counter. Food takes a short airborne route; the chef takes a longer passage route. No dedicated landing or salad station.

The Zigzag Café

Three work areas connect at alternating ends: bottom passage first, top passage second. Throws cut across both low rails.

What the player decides

Send a small batch of raw ingredients to preparation, process it, then send chopped ingredients to service before moving on.

Pantry on the left, chopping in the middle, cooking/assembly/serving on the right. Keep the dish pool on the service side. Reserve some counters for clean dishes instead of filling every surface with ingredients.

Why this level is different

A three-stage zigzag replaces level 3’s single crossing. The second ingredient-transfer step and shared counter space create a new planning problem without adding recipes.

Useful empty surfaces

  • C1–C4: pantry-side staging
  • C5–C9: preparation buffers
  • C10–C13: cooking, assembly and ready dishes

These are suggested uses. Every C-numbered counter accepts ingredients, dishes and assembly in exactly the same way.

Solo pacing

Start with one calm ticket; allow three only after tuning. Test the full dish cycle at walking speed. Barriers stay fixed, passages never close, and a helper is not required.

What the research changed

Phil Duncan describes designing levels around an intended player experience and testing the balance between challenge and frustration. Read the developer interview.

Team17 describes throwing as a way to move ingredients across separated kitchen areas. Read the official guide.

Our adaptation: one chef can always walk between work areas. Throws let the player stage food for their next visit. Counter space and the path between tasks create the decisions.

What these drawings prove

Every shown working face has a walking route. Each recipe has its required stations. All illustrated throws cross a blocked rail cell into an ordinary counter.

They do not yet establish character clearance or good pacing. A solo chef still has to visit the far side after throwing, so the benefit comes from staging a small batch, not removing all travel.
