LEVEL 1 · Pick up · chop · assemble · serve · wash

First Service

A perimeter kitchen leaves the middle open. The two ingredients sit together; the return tray, sink and plate rack form one short washing route.

Tomato–cucumber saladChopped tomato + chopped cucumber
Serve on a clean plate.
8 × 6counter modules4orders to try1active tickets, max.

Starting dish pool: 3 plates · 0 bowls. Wash and reuse; racks do not create new dishes. Order counts are proposals for testing.

How this level plays

  1. Place a clean plate on the salad counter. Chop and add the two ingredients, one at a time.
  2. Serve the salad. After the customer eats, collect the dirty plate, wash it and restock the rack.
  3. Only one ticket is active. The first full loop is an untimed guided practice.

Review focus
Does the short, open route make each station easy to find?

LEVEL 2 · Cooking + dash

Something Simmering

The wider kitchen separates preparation from cooking. A clear horizontal aisle gives dash a useful role, while the sink and bowl rack remain together.

Carrot–mushroom soupChopped carrot + chopped mushroom → cook
Serve in a clean bowl. No separate broth pickup.
10 × 6counter modules6orders to try2active tickets, max.

Starting dish pool: 0 plates · 3 bowls. Wash and reuse; racks do not create new dishes. Order counts are proposals for testing.

How this level plays

  1. Chop carrot and mushroom, then load both into the fixed pot. Cooking starts when both are present.
  2. Use the cooking window to wash a bowl or prepare the next ingredient. Bring a clean bowl to the ready pot, fill it and serve.
  3. Learn dash in the open aisle. Walking must still be sufficient; the cooker stays visible from the washing area.

Review focus
Does the longer aisle make dash useful without making soup preparation feel like a long commute?

LEVEL 3 · Ingredient throw

Lunch Rush

A two-module-deep island separates supply and working aisles. Throws can stage one ingredient at each end for a later visit; they never require a second chef.

Tomato–cucumber saladChopped tomato + chopped cucumber
Serve on a clean plate.
Carrot–mushroom soupChopped carrot + chopped mushroom → cook
Serve in a clean bowl. No separate broth pickup.
11 × 8counter modules8orders to try2active tickets, max.

Starting dish pool: 3 plates · 3 bowls. Wash and reuse; racks do not create new dishes. Order counts are proposals for testing.

How this level plays

  1. Practice one throw into a visibly empty landing counter. Only ingredients can be thrown; dishes and cookware are carried.
  2. A throw carries an ingredient over the low island to the far landing counter. The chef walks around either end to retrieve it.
  3. A highlighted target makes aiming clear. A full target rejects the throw and keeps the ingredient in the chef’s paws.

Review focus
Are the landing counters and the two walking routes around the island obvious?

LEVEL 4 · Combine all learned actions

Café Finale

Two small islands open a central passage. Preparation separates by recipe; serving and dish return meet at the centre so the finale tests priorities with the same familiar kit.

Tomato–cucumber saladChopped tomato + chopped cucumber
Serve on a clean plate.
Carrot–mushroom soupChopped carrot + chopped mushroom → cook
Serve in a clean bowl. No separate broth pickup.
12 × 8counter modules10orders to try3active tickets, max.

Starting dish pool: 3 plates · 3 bowls. Wash and reuse; racks do not create new dishes. Order counts are proposals for testing.

How this level plays

  1. The salad and soup work areas feed the same serving pass. Choose which preparation to finish while the soup cooks.
  2. Use the two-module-wide central passage, the side routes and optional throwing shortcuts. All routes remain open.
  3. A shared sink makes clean-dish planning matter. Increase order overlap gradually; add no new recipe, ingredient or hazard.

Review focus
Does coordinating two recipe areas feel like a satisfying finale for one chef?

States still to specify

Tomato-only and cucumber-only salad assembly; pot with one/both ingredients, cooking, ready and burnt; progress and valid-throw feedback. These reuse existing ingredients and objects.

Reuse across levels

Landing areas are ordinary counters with a target highlight. The soup pot stays on the hob. The return uses a static tray first; an animated belt can use the same endpoint later.

Rules shared by these plans

One chef can do everything

All station interaction edges connect to the walking space. Dash and throws are optional shortcuts. The chef carries one object at a time; route lines show task order, not simultaneous carrying.

A complete dish loop

Serve → customer eats → same dish returns dirty → wash → reuse. Customer seating is a separate presentation area. There is no manual table-delivery step in this first demo.

Drawn, then tested

The plan grid confirms access to every station. Character width, turning space, counter reach, camera visibility, throwing height and pacing still need a Unity blockout after review.
